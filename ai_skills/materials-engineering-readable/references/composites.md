## 1. Polymer Matrix Composites (PMC)

### CFRP — Carbon Fiber Reinforced Polymer

#### Standard Modulus CFRP (Epoxy, Unidirectional)
- **Density:** 1.55–1.60 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength (0°):** 1500–2000 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus (0°):** 130–150 GPa
- **Compressive Strength:** 1000–1200 MPa
- **Flexural Strength:** 1200–1600 MPa
- **Specific Strength:** ~1100 MPa·cm³/g (best of structural materials)
- **Specific Stiffness:** ~90 GPa·cm³/g
- **Fiber Grade:** T300, T700 (Toray) / AS4, IM7 (Hexcel)
- **Max Service Temp:** 120–180°C (depends on matrix)
- **CTE:** 0–2 μm/m·°C (near-zero — excellent dimensional stability)
- **Fatigue Resistance:** Excellent
- **Standard:** ASTM D3039 (tension), D6641 (compression), D790 (flexure)
- **Applications:** Aerospace primary structures, racing cars, robotic arms, pressure vessels, sporting equipment, drone frames
- **Limitations:** Brittle interlaminar failure; galvanic corrosion with aluminum (use barrier layer); difficult to repair; requires autoclave or infusion process; expensive
- **Machining Notes:** Use diamond/CBN tools; carbide OK for short runs; generates hazardous dust — require full respiratory PPE; no coolant flood (delamination risk)
- **Cost Index:** 50–200× carbon steel

#### High-Modulus CFRP (HM)
- **Density:** 1.60–1.65 g/cm³
- **Tensile Modulus:** 200–500 GPa
- **Tensile Strength:** 1000–1500 MPa (lower than SM)
- **Fiber Grade:** M40J, M55J, M60J (Toray)
- **Applications:** Spacecraft, telescope mirrors, precision instruments requiring near-zero CTE and ultra-high stiffness
- **Notes:** Very expensive fiber (5–10× standard modulus); brittle

#### CFRP Quasi-Isotropic Laminate (0/45/90/-45)
- **Density:** 1.55 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 500–700 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus:** 50–70 GPa
- **Notes:** Balanced in-plane properties; most common engineering layup for structural panels

#### CFRP — Short Fiber / Injection Moldable (30% CF)
- **Density:** 1.30–1.40 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 160–220 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus:** 18–25 GPa
- **Max Service Temp:** 120–200°C (depends on matrix — PA, PEEK, PPS)
- **Applications:** Lightweight structural brackets, housings, functional parts via injection molding
- **Key Advantage:** Complex geometry possible; much lighter than aluminum; lower cost than continuous fiber
- **Common Matrices:** PA66-CF30, PPS-CF40, PEEK-CF30

---

### GFRP — Glass Fiber Reinforced Polymer

#### E-Glass / Epoxy (Unidirectional)
- **Density:** 1.90–2.10 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 700–1000 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus:** 40–45 GPa
- **Compressive Strength:** 600–700 MPa
- **Standard:** ASTM D2290, D3039
- **Applications:** Wind turbine blades, boat hulls, structural pipes, pressure vessels, automotive body panels
- **Key vs CFRP:** 3–5× lower cost; electrically insulating; heavier; lower stiffness

#### E-Glass / Polyester (Chopped Strand Mat — CSM)
- **Density:** 1.55–1.70 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 100–200 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus:** 8–15 GPa
- **Applications:** Automotive body panels, boat hulls, tanks, general FRP fabrication
- **Process:** Hand layup, spray-up, RTM
- **Notes:** Lowest cost FRP; isotropic properties; suitable for large non-structural panels

#### S-Glass / Epoxy
- **Density:** 1.90–2.00 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 1700–2100 MPa (fiber) / 1000–1300 MPa (laminate)
- **Tensile Modulus:** 55–60 GPa
- **Key vs E-Glass:** ~30% stronger; better fatigue; higher cost
- **Applications:** Aerospace secondary structures, pressure vessels, ballistic protection (hybrid)

---

### Aramid Composites (Kevlar® / Twaron®)

#### Aramid / Epoxy (Unidirectional)
- **Density:** 1.35–1.40 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 1300–1500 MPa (laminate)
- **Tensile Modulus:** 60–80 GPa
- **Compressive Strength:** 230–280 MPa (POOR — major limitation)
- **Impact Resistance:** Outstanding
- **Applications:** Body armor, helmets, pressure vessels, marine, cut-resistant gloves, aircraft flooring
- **Key Advantage:** Highest specific toughness; excellent vibration damping
- **Limitation:** Very poor compressive strength; absorbs moisture; difficult to machine (fuzzing)

#### Hybrid CFRP/Aramid
- **Density:** ~1.45 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 800–1200 MPa
- **Rationale:** Carbon provides stiffness/compression; aramid provides impact absorption
- **Applications:** Sports equipment, marine, protective panels

---

### Natural Fiber Composites

#### Flax / Epoxy
- **Density:** 1.20–1.30 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 200–280 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus:** 28–40 GPa
- **Applications:** Automotive interior panels, sporting goods (sustainability-driven)
- **Key Advantage:** Bio-based; good vibration damping; lower cost than CFRP

## 2. Metal Matrix Composites (MMC)

### Aluminum Matrix Composites

#### Al / SiC (20% SiCp) — Particle Reinforced
- **Density:** 2.77 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 350–500 MPa
- **Yield Strength:** 310–400 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus:** 97 GPa (vs 69 GPa for Al 6061)
- **Hardness:** 100–130 HB
- **Max Service Temp:** 300°C
- **CTE:** 16.4 μm/m·°C
- **Standard:** ASTM B557 (testing)
- **Applications:** Aerospace brake discs, electronic packaging (PCB heat spreader), automotive pistons and connecting rods, bicycle components
- **Key vs Al 6061:** +40% stiffer, +60% wear resistance, same weight, better thermal stability
- **Machining:** Requires PCD (polycrystalline diamond) tooling — SiC particles destroy carbide rapidly
- **Cost Index:** 15–30× carbon steel

#### Al / SiC (40% SiCp)
- **Density:** 2.90–2.96 g/cm³
- **Tensile Modulus:** 150 GPa
- **CTE:** 10.5 μm/m·°C (closer to silicon/GaAs for electronic packaging)
- **Applications:** Electronic heat sinks, microwave packaging, space structures
- **Key Advantage:** Near-zero CTE matching semiconductor substrates

#### Al / Al₂O₃ (Alumina Particle)
- **Density:** 2.75–2.90 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 300–450 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus:** 85–110 GPa
- **Applications:** Automotive pistons, brake rotors, wear parts
- **Key vs Al/SiC:** Lower cost; can use carbide tooling (but still with care)

### Titanium Matrix Composites

#### Ti / TiC (Particle Reinforced)
- **Density:** 4.35–4.50 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 900–1100 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus:** 145 GPa
- **Max Service Temp:** 600°C
- **Applications:** Aerospace structures, high-temp engine components
- **Notes:** Very expensive; processed by powder metallurgy

### Copper Matrix Composites

#### Cu / W (80% W — Tungsten Copper)
- **Density:** 15.0–17.0 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 500–600 MPa
- **Thermal Conductivity:** 180–200 W/m·K
- **CTE:** 7.0–9.0 μm/m·°C (matches kovar, steel)
- **Applications:** Electrical contacts, EDM electrodes, heat sinks for high-power electronics, spot welding electrodes
- **Key Advantage:** High conductivity + low CTE → ideal thermal management interface

## 3. Ceramic Matrix Composites (CMC)

### C/C — Carbon-Carbon Composite
- **Density:** 1.60–1.95 g/cm³
- **Flexural Strength:** 100–300 MPa
- **Tensile Modulus:** 60–150 GPa
- **Max Service Temp:** 3000°C (inert/vacuum) / 500°C (air — oxidizes rapidly)
- **CTE:** 1.0–2.0 μm/m·°C
- **Thermal Conductivity:** 150–400 W/m·K (fiber direction)
- **Applications:** Rocket nozzles, spacecraft reentry heat shields, aircraft/F1 brake discs, furnace fixtures
- **Key Property:** Retains strength at extreme temperatures; gets stronger when heated (to ~2500°C)
- **Limitation:** Oxidizes in air above 500°C — needs SiC coating or protective atmosphere

### SiC/SiC — Silicon Carbide Fiber in SiC Matrix
- **Density:** 2.70–3.00 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 300–500 MPa
- **Max Service Temp:** 1400°C (air)
- **Key vs Nickel Superalloy:** 30% lower density; same or better temperature capability
- **Applications:** Aircraft engine hot section (GE9X turbine blades use SiC/SiC), industrial gas turbines
- **Notes:** Replacing Inconel in some aero applications for weight/temp benefits; extremely expensive

## 4. Advanced & Hybrid Materials

### Sandwich Structures

#### CFRP Face / Nomex® Honeycomb Core
- **Density (panel):** 0.05–0.30 g/cm³ (ultra-light)
- **Flexural Stiffness-to-Weight:** Extremely high
- **Applications:** Aircraft interior panels, racing car tubs, satellite structures, wind turbine nacelles
- **Principle:** Thin strong faces carry bending load; light core provides separation for high section modulus

#### Al Face / Aluminum Honeycomb Core
- **Density (panel):** 0.08–0.40 g/cm³
- **Applications:** Aircraft floors, train interiors, tooling plates, clean room floors

### Glare® (Glass Laminate Aluminum Reinforced Epoxy)
- **Density:** 2.38 g/cm³
- **Tensile Strength:** 400–600 MPa
- **Fatigue Resistance:** Excellent (self-healing due to metal layers)
- **Applications:** Airbus A380 fuselage upper panels, aircraft engine cowlings
- **Key Advantage:** Better fatigue + damage tolerance than monolithic aluminum; lighter than steel

## 5. Substitution Guide

### CFRP Substitutions
| Need | Original | Substitute | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost reduction | CFRP (UD) | GFRP (UD) | 4–5× heavier per stiffness unit; 5× cheaper |
| Cost reduction | CFRP (UD) | Al 7075-T6 | 1.8× heavier; much easier to repair and machine |
| Complex geometry | CFRP (laminate) | CF-PA66 (injection) | Lower performance; complex shapes possible |
| Sustainability | CFRP | Flax/Epoxy | Much lower performance; bio-based |
| Impact resistance | CFRP | CFRP/Aramid hybrid | Better impact; slightly lower stiffness |

### MMC Substitutions
| Need | Original | Substitute | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost reduction | Al/SiC MMC | Al 7075-T6 | Lower stiffness and wear; 3–5× cheaper |
| Higher stiffness | Al 6061-T6 | Al/SiC (20%) | +40% modulus; requires PCD machining |
| Lower CTE | Al/SiC (20%) | Al/SiC (40%) | Higher density; better CTE match for electronics |

### Composite vs Metal
| Scenario | Metal | Composite | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight critical, budget available | Ti-6Al-4V | CFRP (UD) | CFRP if stiffness-critical |
| Weight critical, budget tight | Al 7075 | GFRP or CF-PA66 | Al 7075 if machinability matters |
| High temp (>300°C) | Inconel 718 | — (limited options) | Metal wins; CMC if budget allows |
| Complex shape, medium load | Al (die cast) | CF-PA66 (inj. mold) | Composite if weight is priority |
| High wear | Steel / WC | Al/SiC MMC | MMC for weight + wear combo |
