## Technical Ceramics

### Alumina (Al₂O₃)

#### 99.5% Alumina
**Classification**: Oxide ceramic  
**Density**: 3.89 g/cm³  
**Purity**: 99.5% Al₂O₃

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Tensile Strength: 260-300 MPa
- Compressive Strength: 2500-3000 MPa
- Flexural Strength: 350-400 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 370-380 GPa
- Hardness: 1440-1650 HV (Vickers)
- Fracture Toughness (KIC): 4-5 MPa√m
- Poisson's Ratio: 0.21-0.27

**Thermal Properties**:
- Melting Point: 2050°C
- Max Service Temperature: 1700°C (oxidizing), 1500°C (reducing)
- Thermal Conductivity: 30-35 W/m·K (room temp)
- Thermal Expansion: 7.5-8.5 μm/m·K
- Thermal Shock Resistance: Moderate

**Electrical Properties**:
- Dielectric Constant (1 MHz): 9.5-10.0
- Dielectric Strength: 10-35 kV/mm
- Volume Resistivity (25°C): >10¹⁴ Ω·cm
- Loss Tangent: 0.0002-0.0005

**Characteristics**:
- Excellent hardness and wear resistance
- High temperature stability
- Excellent electrical insulation
- Good chemical resistance (acids, alkalis at elevated temp)
- Biocompatible
- Brittle (low fracture toughness)
- Difficult to machine
- Good optical properties (translucent to transparent when thin)

**Typical Applications**: Cutting tools, wear components, electrical insulators, spark plug insulators, crucibles, ballistic armor, biomedical implants (hip/knee replacements)

#### 96% Alumina
**Density**: 3.72 g/cm³  
**Purity**: 96% Al₂O₃

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Flexural Strength: 300-350 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 300-320 GPa
- Hardness: 1200-1400 HV

**Thermal Properties**:
- Max Service Temperature: 1600°C
- Thermal Conductivity: 20-25 W/m·K

**Characteristics**:
- Lower cost than 99.5% alumina
- Good balance of properties
- Slightly lower mechanical and thermal properties
- Easier to process than higher purity grades

**Typical Applications**: Industrial wear parts, electrical components, metallization substrates

#### 85% Alumina
**Density**: 3.50 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Flexural Strength: 250-300 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 260-300 GPa

**Characteristics**:
- Most economical alumina grade
- Good for less demanding applications
- Easier to machine than higher purity grades

### Zirconia (ZrO₂)

#### Yttria-Stabilized Zirconia (3Y-TZP)
**Classification**: Oxide ceramic (partially stabilized)  
**Density**: 6.05 g/cm³  
**Composition**: ZrO₂ with 3 mol% Y₂O₃

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Flexural Strength: 900-1200 MPa (exceptionally high for ceramic!)
- Compressive Strength: 2000-2500 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 200-210 GPa
- Hardness: 1200-1300 HV
- Fracture Toughness (KIC): 6-10 MPa√m (best for ceramics)
- Weibull Modulus: 10-15

**Thermal Properties**:
- Melting Point: 2715°C
- Max Service Temperature: 1000°C
- Thermal Conductivity: 2-3 W/m·K (very low)
- Thermal Expansion: 10-11 μm/m·K
- Thermal Shock Resistance: Good

**Electrical Properties**:
- Dielectric Constant: 25-30
- Ionic Conductor at high temperature (oxygen ions)

**Characteristics**:
- Highest flexural strength of technical ceramics
- Excellent fracture toughness (transformation toughening)
- Good wear resistance
- Biocompatible
- Chemically inert
- Low thermal conductivity (thermal barrier)
- White, opaque appearance (can be polished to high gloss)
- Susceptible to low temperature degradation (LTD) in humid environments
- Phase transformation phenomenon provides toughening

**Typical Applications**: Dental implants/crowns, biomedical implants, cutting tools, bearings, pump components, thermal barrier coatings, oxygen sensors

### Silicon Carbide (SiC)

#### Sintered Silicon Carbide (SSiC)
**Classification**: Non-oxide ceramic  
**Density**: 3.10 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Flexural Strength: 350-550 MPa
- Compressive Strength: 2500-3900 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 410-450 GPa (extremely high)
- Hardness: 2400-2800 HV (one of hardest materials)
- Fracture Toughness (KIC): 3-5 MPa√m

**Thermal Properties**:
- Melting Point: Sublimes at 2700°C
- Max Service Temperature: 1400-1650°C (oxidizing)
- Thermal Conductivity: 100-120 W/m·K (excellent for ceramic)
- Thermal Expansion: 4.0-4.5 μm/m·K (low)
- Thermal Shock Resistance: Excellent

**Electrical Properties**:
- Semiconductor behavior
- Can be electrically conductive (depending on dopants)

**Characteristics**:
- Exceptional hardness (third hardest after diamond and boron carbide)
- Excellent wear resistance
- High thermal conductivity for a ceramic
- Excellent thermal shock resistance
- Good chemical resistance
- Low density
- Oxidation resistant up to 1650°C
- Expensive
- Difficult to machine

**Typical Applications**: Mechanical seals, bearings, nozzles, heat exchangers, armor, semiconductor processing equipment, diesel particulate filters

#### Reaction-Bonded Silicon Carbide (RBSiC)
**Density**: 3.05-3.10 g/cm³

**Characteristics**:
- Contains some free silicon (~10-15%)
- Lower strength than SSiC
- Easier to form complex shapes
- Lower cost than SSiC
- Good thermal shock resistance

### Silicon Nitride (Si₃N₄)
**Classification**: Non-oxide ceramic  
**Density**: 3.20-3.30 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Flexural Strength: 600-1000 MPa
- Compressive Strength: 3000-3500 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 300-320 GPa
- Hardness: 1400-1900 HV
- Fracture Toughness (KIC): 6-8 MPa√m (excellent)

**Thermal Properties**:
- Melting Point: Decomposes at 1900°C
- Max Service Temperature: 1200-1400°C
- Thermal Conductivity: 15-30 W/m·K
- Thermal Expansion: 2.5-3.5 μm/m·K
- Thermal Shock Resistance: Excellent

**Characteristics**:
- Excellent high-temperature strength (retains strength at 1000°C)
- Excellent thermal shock resistance
- Good fracture toughness
- Good wear resistance
- Oxidation resistant
- Creep resistant
- Lightweight
- Difficult to sinter (requires sintering aids)
- Expensive

**Typical Applications**: Turbine blades, cutting tools, bearings, engine components, welding fixtures

## Glass Materials

### Soda-Lime Glass
**Classification**: Silicate glass  
**Density**: 2.52 g/cm³  
**Composition**: ~70% SiO₂, 15% Na₂O, 9% CaO

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Tensile Strength: 40-70 MPa
- Compressive Strength: 500-1000 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 70 GPa
- Hardness: 5-6 Mohs, 550 HV
- Fracture Toughness (KIC): 0.7-0.8 MPa√m

**Thermal Properties**:
- Softening Point: 696-726°C
- Annealing Point: 514-544°C
- Max Service Temperature: ~200-250°C
- Thermal Conductivity: 0.8-1.0 W/m·K
- Thermal Expansion: 9 μm/m·K
- Thermal Shock Resistance: Poor

**Optical Properties**:
- Refractive Index: 1.51-1.52
- Transmission: Excellent visible light, poor UV/IR

**Characteristics**:
- Lowest cost glass
- Good chemical resistance (except acids)
- Easy to form and shape
- Recyclable
- Brittle
- Poor thermal shock resistance
- Attacked by hydrofluoric acid

**Typical Applications**: Windows, bottles, containers, tableware, light bulbs

### Borosilicate Glass (Pyrex®, Duran®)
**Classification**: Silicate glass  
**Density**: 2.23 g/cm³  
**Composition**: 70-80% SiO₂, 7-13% B₂O₃, 4-8% Na₂O, 2-7% Al₂O₃

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Tensile Strength: 40-70 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 63 GPa
- Hardness: 5.5-6.5 Mohs, 480 HV
- Fracture Toughness (KIC): 0.7-0.9 MPa√m

**Thermal Properties**:
- Softening Point: 820°C
- Max Service Temperature: 230-500°C (grade dependent)
- Thermal Conductivity: 1.14 W/m·K
- Thermal Expansion: 3.3 μm/m·K (very low!)
- Thermal Shock Resistance: Excellent

**Optical Properties**:
- Refractive Index: 1.47-1.48
- Good UV transmission

**Characteristics**:
- Excellent thermal shock resistance (low expansion)
- Excellent chemical resistance (acids and alkalis)
- Higher temperature capability than soda-lime
- Good optical clarity
- More expensive than soda-lime glass
- Withstands rapid temperature changes

**Typical Applications**: Laboratory glassware, cookware, chemical processing, pharmaceutical packaging, telescope mirrors, lighting

### Fused Silica / Quartz Glass
**Classification**: Pure silica glass  
**Density**: 2.20 g/cm³  
**Composition**: >99.95% SiO₂

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Tensile Strength: 50-110 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 73 GPa
- Hardness: 7 Mohs, 600 HV
- Fracture Toughness (KIC): 0.7-0.9 MPa√m

**Thermal Properties**:
- Softening Point: 1670°C
- Max Service Temperature: 900-1200°C (continuous)
- Thermal Conductivity: 1.38 W/m·K
- Thermal Expansion: 0.5 μm/m·K (extremely low!)
- Thermal Shock Resistance: Outstanding

**Optical Properties**:
- Refractive Index: 1.458
- Excellent UV transmission (down to 180 nm)
- Low absorption in UV/IR

**Characteristics**:
- Outstanding thermal shock resistance
- Excellent optical properties (UV transparency)
- Excellent purity
- Excellent chemical resistance
- High temperature capability
- Low thermal expansion
- Very expensive
- Difficult to work with

**Typical Applications**: Semiconductor processing, optics (lenses, prisms), UV lamps, high-temp laboratory ware, fiber optics, telescope mirrors

### Gorilla Glass (Chemically Strengthened)
**Classification**: Alkali-aluminosilicate glass  
**Density**: 2.54 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Surface Compressive Stress: 700-900 MPa (ion-exchange strengthened)
- Flexural Strength: 500-700 MPa
- Elastic Modulus: 75 GPa
- Hardness: 622 HV

**Characteristics**:
- Ion-exchange strengthened (surface compression)
- Excellent scratch resistance
- High damage resistance
- Thin and lightweight
- Good optical clarity
- Expensive

**Typical Applications**: Smartphone screens, tablet screens, touchscreens, wearables

## Fiber-Reinforced Composites

### Carbon Fiber / Epoxy (CF/Epoxy)

#### Standard Modulus Carbon Fiber Composite
**Fiber Volume Fraction**: 50-60%  
**Density**: 1.55-1.60 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties** (Unidirectional, longitudinal):
- Tensile Strength: 1200-2500 MPa
- Tensile Modulus: 120-150 GPa
- Compressive Strength: 850-1200 MPa
- Flexural Strength: 1000-1500 MPa
- Shear Strength: 80-120 MPa
- Elongation at Break: 1.0-1.8%

**Mechanical Properties** (Quasi-isotropic layup [0/±45/90]):
- Tensile Strength: 400-600 MPa
- Tensile Modulus: 50-65 GPa

**Thermal Properties**:
- Glass Transition Temperature (Tg): 120-180°C (epoxy dependent)
- Max Service Temperature: 120-150°C (continuous)
- Thermal Expansion: -0.5 to 1.0 μm/m·K (longitudinal), 30 μm/m·K (transverse)
- Thermal Conductivity: 3-10 W/m·K (highly anisotropic)

**Characteristics**:
- Excellent strength-to-weight ratio (5x steel by weight)
- Excellent stiffness-to-weight ratio (2x steel by weight)
- Excellent fatigue resistance
- Low thermal expansion (longitudinal)
- Good damping properties
- Corrosion resistant
- Anisotropic properties (direction-dependent)
- Electrically conductive
- Susceptible to impact damage (brittle)
- Very expensive
- Specialized manufacturing processes required
- Cannot be recycled easily
- Poor transverse properties (matrix-dominated)

**Typical Applications**: Aerospace structures, racing cars, bicycles, sporting equipment, wind turbine blades, pressure vessels

#### High-Modulus Carbon Fiber Composite
**Density**: 1.60-1.65 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties** (Unidirectional, longitudinal):
- Tensile Modulus: 220-640 GPa (ultra-high stiffness)
- Tensile Strength: 1500-2500 MPa

**Characteristics**:
- Higher stiffness than standard modulus
- Lower strain to failure
- More brittle
- Even more expensive

**Typical Applications**: Aerospace, satellites, precision instruments, Formula 1

### Glass Fiber / Polyester (GF/Polyester)

#### E-Glass / Polyester
**Fiber Volume Fraction**: 30-50%  
**Density**: 1.80-2.10 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties** (Chopped strand mat):
- Tensile Strength: 60-150 MPa
- Tensile Modulus: 7-15 GPa
- Flexural Strength: 120-250 MPa

**Mechanical Properties** (Woven roving):
- Tensile Strength: 150-350 MPa
- Tensile Modulus: 15-25 GPa
- Flexural Strength: 200-400 MPa

**Thermal Properties**:
- Max Service Temperature: 60-120°C (polyester resin dependent)
- Thermal Expansion: 20-30 μm/m·K

**Characteristics**:
- Good strength-to-weight ratio (but lower than carbon fiber)
- Lower cost than carbon fiber (10-20x cheaper)
- Good corrosion resistance
- Electrical insulator (non-conductive)
- Easy to process (hand layup, spray-up, RTM)
- Transparent to radar (radome applications)
- Lower stiffness than carbon fiber
- Higher density than carbon fiber
- Moisture absorption (resin dependent)
- UV degradation (requires gel coat or paint)

**Typical Applications**: Boats, marine structures, automotive body panels, storage tanks, pipes, bathtubs, wind turbine blades (low-cost)

### Aramid Fiber / Epoxy (Kevlar / Epoxy)
**Fiber Volume Fraction**: 50-60%  
**Density**: 1.38-1.44 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties** (Unidirectional, longitudinal):
- Tensile Strength: 1200-1800 MPa
- Tensile Modulus: 70-130 GPa
- Compressive Strength: 280-350 MPa (poor!)
- Shear Strength: 30-50 MPa
- Elongation at Break: 2.5-4.0%

**Thermal Properties**:
- Max Service Temperature: 150-180°C
- Decomposition Temperature: >400°C
- Thermal Expansion: -2 to -6 μm/m·K (negative!)

**Characteristics**:
- Excellent tensile strength-to-weight ratio
- Excellent impact resistance and toughness
- Excellent ballistic protection
- Good fatigue resistance
- Excellent vibration damping
- Electrical insulator
- Poor compressive strength
- Poor shear strength
- Difficult to machine (fuzzy edges)
- UV degradation (requires protection)
- Moisture absorption
- Cannot withstand high temperatures

**Typical Applications**: Ballistic protection (body armor, helmets), aerospace components, sporting goods, ropes, cables, brake pads, cut-resistant gloves

### Carbon Fiber / PEEK
**Fiber Volume Fraction**: 50-60%  
**Density**: 1.53-1.60 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties** (Unidirectional, longitudinal):
- Tensile Strength: 1500-2200 MPa
- Tensile Modulus: 130-160 GPa
- Compressive Strength: 900-1300 MPa

**Thermal Properties**:
- Glass Transition (Tg): 143°C
- Melting Point: 343°C
- Max Service Temperature: 240°C (continuous)

**Characteristics**:
- High-temperature thermoplastic matrix
- Excellent chemical resistance
- Can be thermoformed and reshaped
- Recyclable/repairable (thermoplastic)
- Excellent impact resistance
- Biocompatible
- Very expensive (matrix + fiber + processing)
- Requires high processing temperatures (380-400°C)

**Typical Applications**: Aerospace structural parts, oil & gas, medical implants, high-performance automotive

## Advanced Materials

### Shape Memory Alloys

#### Nitinol (Nickel-Titanium)
**Composition**: ~55% Ni, 45% Ti (near-equiatomic)  
**Density**: 6.45 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties** (Superelastic, 37°C):
- Tensile Strength: 760-1900 MPa (condition dependent)
- Yield Strength: 195-690 MPa
- Elongation: 15-50%
- Elastic Modulus: 28-83 GPa (Austenite), 28-41 GPa (Martensite)
- Superelastic Strain: Up to 8% (recoverable)

**Transformation Temperatures** (vary with composition/processing):
- Austenite Start (As): -50 to +100°C
- Austenite Finish (Af): -30 to +110°C
- Martensite Start (Ms): -100 to +70°C
- Martensite Finish (Mf): -120 to +50°C

**Characteristics**:
- Shape memory effect (SME) - recovers original shape when heated
- Superelasticity - recovers large strains (up to 8%)
- Good biocompatibility
- Excellent corrosion resistance
- High damping capacity
- Kink resistant
- Expensive
- Complex processing
- Training required for shape setting
- Temperature-dependent properties

**Typical Applications**: Medical devices (stents, guidewires, orthodontic wires), actuators, couplings, eyeglass frames, antennas

### Piezoelectric Ceramics

#### PZT (Lead Zirconate Titanate)
**Composition**: Pb(Zr,Ti)O₃  
**Density**: 7.5-8.0 g/cm³

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Elastic Modulus: 60-120 GPa
- Tensile Strength: 60-100 MPa

**Electrical Properties**:
- Piezoelectric Coefficient (d₃₃): 200-600 pC/N
- Curie Temperature: 250-390°C
- Dielectric Constant: 1200-3800
- Coupling Coefficient: 0.70-0.75

**Characteristics**:
- High piezoelectric effect
- Electromechanical coupling
- Converts mechanical stress to electrical charge (and vice versa)
- Poling required for piezoelectric properties
- Brittle ceramic
- Lead content (environmental concern)

**Typical Applications**: Sensors (pressure, acceleration), actuators (precision positioning), ultrasonic transducers, sonar, igniters, buzzers

### Aerogels

#### Silica Aerogel
**Density**: 0.003-0.35 g/cm³ (typical: 0.10 g/cm³)

**Thermal Properties**:
- Thermal Conductivity: 0.010-0.020 W/m·K (one of lowest)
- Max Service Temperature: 650°C (silica-based)

**Mechanical Properties**:
- Compressive Strength: 0.003-3 MPa (very weak)
- Elastic Modulus: 0.001-0.1 GPa

**Characteristics**:
- Lowest density solid material (99.8% air)
- Excellent thermal insulation (best insulator)
- Transparent or translucent
- High surface area (600-1000 m²/g)
- Brittle and fragile
- Hydrophobic (surface-treated)
- Expensive

**Typical Applications**: Aerospace insulation, windows (translucent insulation), oil spill cleanup, catalysis, particle detectors

### Graphene
**Density**: 2.267 g/cm³ (single layer)

**Mechanical Properties** (Single layer):
- Tensile Strength: ~130 GPa (strongest material known!)
- Elastic Modulus: ~1 TPa (1000 GPa)
- Breaking Strength: 42 N/m

**Electrical Properties**:
- Electrical Conductivity: Very high (10⁶ S/m)
- Electron Mobility: >200,000 cm²/(V·s)

**Thermal Properties**:
- Thermal Conductivity: 3000-5000 W/m·K (highest known)

**Characteristics**:
- Single layer of carbon atoms (2D material)
- Strongest material per unit weight
- Excellent electrical and thermal conductor
- Transparent (97.7% light transmission)
- Impermeable to gases
- Currently difficult to produce in large quantities
- Very expensive
- Integration challenges into practical devices

**Potential Applications**: Electronics (transistors, sensors), composites, energy storage (batteries, supercapacitors), water filtration, flexible displays
